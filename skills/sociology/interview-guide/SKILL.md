# Interview Guide

Input: a research question (or a research brief) and who will be interviewed. Output: an interview guide you can take into the room, with open questions grouped by theme, follow-up probes, timing and consent points, already checked for bias.

## How to work

1. **Clarify in one message.** Ask:
   - **Research question:** what the interviews must help answer (a brief from research-question-grill works)
   - **Participants:** who they are, how they're recruited, and anything sensitive about them (minors, vulnerable groups, people talking about their employer)
   - **Format:** length, in person or remote, one-to-one or a focus group, language
   - **Analysis plan:** e.g. thematic analysis. This decides how structured the guide should be.

   Done when you know the question, the participants and the length.

2. **Turn the question into themes.** Break the research question into 3–6 themes that the interview must cover. Map each theme back to a sub-question. Done when every sub-question has at least one theme.

3. **Write the questions.** For each theme, write 2–4 open main questions ("Tell me about…", "Walk me through the last time…"), each with 1–3 probes ("What happened next?", "Can you give an example?", "How did that feel?"). Ask about concrete experiences before opinions. Done when the guide fits the time with about 20% to spare.

4. **Bias check.** Check every question and fix any that are: leading ("Don't you think…?"), double-barreled (two questions in one), loaded or judgmental, yes/no when you want a story, full of jargon, or too abstract. Done when you can list every change.

5. **Add the frame:** an introduction and consent script, warm-up questions, a closing ("Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?"), and notes for the interviewer.

## Output template

```markdown
# Interview guide: <study>

**Research question:** ... · **Participants:** ... · **Length:** ... · **Format:** ...

## Before the interview
- Consent points: purpose, recording, anonymity, right to skip or stop, data storage
- Introduction script (2–3 sentences)

## Warm-up (≈5 min)
1. ...

## Theme 1: <name> (≈ x min) — answers sub-question 1
1. <main question>
   - Probe: ...
   - Probe: ...

## Theme 2 ...

## Closing (≈5 min)
- Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?

## Interviewer notes
- Sensitive moments and how to handle them
- Bias-check changes made: <before → after>
```

## Rules

- Open questions first; ask about specific experiences before general opinions.
- Never put the hypothesis in the question.
- For sensitive topics, include a line on what to do if a participant becomes distressed, and remind the user to check their ethics approval requirements.
